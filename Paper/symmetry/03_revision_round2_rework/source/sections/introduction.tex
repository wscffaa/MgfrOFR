\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Old films preserve cinematic heritage and historical documentary material and form large archival collections, yet the film stock that stores this material continues to decay. Digital restoration offers a practical route to revitalize such material~\cite{iizuka2019deepremaster}. A central difficulty is that aged footage is rarely damaged in only one way. Scratches, dust, blotches, flicker, blur, grain, and fading can accumulate within the same clip and act on very different spatial scales, from large structural damage to thin local artifacts. This range of scales sets old film restoration apart from generic video enhancement. A successful restoration model must keep coarse structural recovery, regional texture repair, and fine artifact suppression in balance---a representation-level symmetry that asymmetric, heterogeneous degradation continually disrupts.

Learning-based old film restoration has advanced through end-to-end video restoration frameworks. RTN pioneered bidirectional recurrent propagation with a learnable defect mask, but its restoration evidence still flows through a single recurrent stream~\cite{wan2022rtn}. RRTN added recursive transformer propagation for stronger temporal alignment, yet structural recovery and fine artifact suppression remain jointly encoded~\cite{lin2024rrtn}. MambaOFR introduced state space models to old film restoration, improving efficiency and establishing the Synthetic/Real-World Old Video (SRWOV) benchmark~\cite{mao2025mambaofr,gu2024mamba,guo2024mambair}. Even so, it routes every degradation scale through a single propagated feature pathway. These methods share a common limitation: when heterogeneous signals compete in one latent space, coarse recovery can overwrite fine evidence, and local artifact suppression can distort global structure.

Here, symmetry operates at the representation level rather than as an object-level shape constraint. A symmetric representation keeps restoration evidence balanced across granularity-specific branches while maintaining consistent temporal correspondence across frames. Because our branch-diversity objective is invariant under permutations of the granularity branches (the symmetric group $\mathcal{S}_G$), this balance is a permutation symmetry in the machine-learning sense---a standard group-symmetry principle---rather than a visible geometric one, placing MgfrOFR within symmetry-driven representation learning for image restoration. This interpretation fits old film restoration because its degradation space is hierarchical. Missing regions and large blotches require global context, grain and flicker require regional modeling, and fine scratches or dust require local precision. Existing multi-granularity designs mainly target detection or recognition~\cite{lin2017fpn,wang2021hrnet,sun2018pcb,wang2018mgn,gu2025reidmamba}. Naive multi-branch extensions can still collapse into correlated features unless branch diversity is explicitly enforced. This gap motivates explicit granularity-separated restoration branches.

We propose MgfrOFR, which augments MambaOFR with two complementary components (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). The Multi-Granularity Feature Extractor (MGFE) reorganizes propagated Mamba features into coarse-to-fine restoration branches, so that structural completion, regional repair, and fine-detail suppression are carried by dedicated descriptors instead of competing in one hidden stream. It also feeds branch summaries back into the spatial stream through channel-wise recalibration. Ranking-Aware Triplet Regularization (RATR) complements this decomposition by discouraging redundant branch behavior: it penalizes correlated rankings of temporal correspondences rather than imposing identity-label supervision, making the regularizer suited to restoration.

Using a unified reproduction protocol on SRWOV, MgfrOFR preserves the efficient Mamba backbone of MambaOFR while adding explicit granularity control at modest overhead. The main contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We formulate old film restoration through representation-level symmetry under heterogeneous degradation, treating coarse structural recovery, regional texture repair, and fine artifact suppression as complementary feature components that remain balanced despite spatial and temporal asymmetry.
    \item We design MGFE, a multi-granularity feature decomposition for Mamba-based old film restoration that inserts learnable restoration tokens into propagated features, aggregates them into coarse-to-fine branches, and feeds branch summaries back to the spatial stream through channel-wise recalibration.
    \item We introduce RATR, a ranking-aware triplet regularizer that penalizes correlated temporal-similarity rankings across granularity branches using differentiable rank correlation, improving branch complementarity and reducing redundancy while preserving the original baseline inference interface.
    \item We conduct a unified evaluation on the SRWOV benchmark covering synthetic and real-world archival footage, where statistical tests, ablation studies, and complexity analysis show that MgfrOFR improves restoration fidelity and no-reference naturalness at modest additional computational cost.
\end{enumerate}
